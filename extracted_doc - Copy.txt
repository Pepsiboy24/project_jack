CHAPTER ONE
INTRODUCTION
1.1 Background of the study   
The rapid expansion of the digital economy has transformed how customers discover and purchase products online. Today, many e-commerce platforms use recommendation systems to help users find products that match their interest and preferences. These systems analyze users’ data such as browsing history, previous purchases, and product ratings to suggest items that a user may like (Jensen & Abbas,2024). Recommendation systems are important because they help users discover products easily while also helping businesses increase customer engagement and sales (Javaid et al.,2022).  
As online platforms continue to grow, the amount of data generated by users has also increased. To handle this large amount of data, many e-commerce platforms now use advanced machine learning models to improve the accuracy of their recommendation (Kumar & Singh,2023). These models can analyze between users and products, making it possible to generate more personalized suggestions. However, many of these advanced models work as black-box systems, meaning that it is difficult for users and developers to understand how the system arrived at a particular recommendation (Saeed & Omlin,2023).
The lack of transparency in black-box systems has become a major concern in modern recommendation systems. When users receive products suggestions without understanding the reason behind them, they may not fully trust the system (Olusegun,2025). In addition, developers may find it difficult to detect errors or biases in the recommendation model because the internal decision-making process is not clear (Raulino et al.,2024). This problem makes it important to develop systems that can explain how recommendations are generated.
Explainable Artificial Intelligence (XAI) is a field of study that focuses on making artificial intelligence systems easier for humans to understand. XAI helps provide explanations that shows why a system made a particular decision or predictions (Javaid et al.,2022). In the case of recommendation systems to tell users why a particular product was suggested. For example, the system may explain that a product was recommended because the user previously bought similar items in that category (Jensen & Abbas,2024).
Researchers have developed different techniques to explain the decision made by complex machine learning models. Some popular methods include Local Interpretable Model-Agnostic Explanations (LIME) and SHapley Addictive Explanations (SHAP). These techniques help identify which factors influenced the recommendation made by the system (Molnar,2022). Studies show that SHAP often provides more detailed explanations, while LIME can generate explanations more quickly, which makes it useful for real-time systems (Lundberg & Lee,2017).
Even though many explanations techniques have been developed, a major challenge is that the explanations are sometimes too technical for ordinary users to understand. Many systems focuses more on the technical explanations in a way that users can easily understand (Adadi & Berrada,2018). Because of this, researchers are now focusing on user centric explainable AI, which aims to provide explanations that are simple, clear and useful for everyday users (Olusegun,2025).
In addition, there is increasing attention on the need for transparency in AI systems. Many experts believe that AI systems should be able to explain their decisions, especially when those decisions influence users’ choices and behavior (Kumar & Singh,2023). In e-commerce platforms, recommendation systems can influence what products users buy, making it important for these systems to be transparent and trustworthy (Jensen & Abbas,2024). 
Therefore, there is a need to develop recommendations systems that not only provide accurate suggestions but also explain those suggestions in a way that users can understand. This research focuses on the development of a user centric explainable AI framework for black-box e-commerce recommendation systems. The aim is to design a system that can generate clear and understandable explanations for product recommendations. By improving transparency, the system can help increase users trust and improve the overall experience of using e-commerce platforms. 
1.2 Statement of the Problem 
Recommendation systems have become an important part of modern e-commerce platforms because they help users find products that match their interests and preferences. These systems analyze users’ data such as browsing history, purchase records, and product interactions in order to provide personalized recommendations (Jensen & Abbas, 2024). As a result, many online businesses rely heavily on recommendation systems to improve customer experience and increase sales.
However, many modern recommendation systems use complex machine learning models that operate as black-box systems. In such systems, it is difficult for users and developers to clearly understand how the system generates its recommendations (Saeed & Omlin,2023). Although these models may provide accurate predictions, the lack of transparency makes it difficult to explain why a particular product is recommended to a user. 
This lack of explainability creates several challenges. First, users may not fully trust recommendations when they do not understand the reason behind them (Olusegun,2025). Second, developers may find it difficult to detect errors, biases, or unfair recommendations because the internal processes of the system are not easily interpretable (Raulino et al.,2024). As the use of artificial intelligence continues to increase in digital platforms, the demand for systems that are transparent and understandable has also grown.
Although different explainable artificial intelligence (XAI) techniques such as LIME and SHAP have been developed to interpret machine learning models, many existing solutions still focus mainly on technical explanations that are easy for ordinary users to understand (Monlar, 2023). As a result, users may still find it difficult to interpret the explanations provided by these systems. 
Therefore, the main the problem addressed in this study is the lack of user-friendly explanations in black-box e-commerce recommendation system. There is a need to develop a user centric explainable AI framework that can provide clear and understandable explanations for recommendations generated by complex machine learning models. Such a framework can improve transparency, increase trust, and enhance the overall usability of recommendation system in e-commerce platforms.
1.3 Aim and Objectives 
The aim of this study is to develop a user a user-centric explainable artificial intelligence (XAI) framework for black-box e-commerce recommendation systems in order to provide clear and understandable explanations for products recommendations.
Objectives
Examine the limitations of existing black-box recommendations systems in terms of transparency and explainability.
Design a user-centric explainable AI framework that can provide understandable explanations for recommendations generated by a machine learning models.
Implement the proposed framework using appropriate machine learning and explainability techniques.
Evaluate the effectiveness of the framework in improving transparency and user understanding of recommendations. 
1.4 Significance of the Study 
The development of a user-centric Explainable Artificial Intelligence (XAI) framework for black-box e-commerce recommendation systems has both theoretical and practical importance. As digital retail industry increasingly relies on complex machine learning models, this study helps improve the balance between recommendation accuracy and human understanding. The significance of this research can be viewed in three main areas:
Practical Significance for E-commerce Platforms and Developers: 
For the e-commerce industry and engagement often depend on the effectiveness of recommendation systems. By integrating an explainable AI framework, business can improve user trust in the recommendations provided by the system. When users understand why a product is recommended, they are more likely to interact with the platform and accept the recommendation. In addition, the software developers and data scientists, the framework can help improve system transparency by showing the factors that influence recommendations. This can help developers identify possible biases, detect system errors, and improve the performance of recommendation model.
Significance for End Users (Consumers):
From the user’s perspective, this study addresses the issue of trust in AI-based recommendation systems. Many users are often unsure why certain products are often recommended to them. By providing clear explanations in simple text or visual to form, the framework can help users understand the reason behind product suggestion. This transparency can reduce confusion, improve user confidence in the system, and help users make more informed purchasing decisions. 
Academic Contribution to Computer Science and AI Research:
Academically, this study contributes to the growing field of Explainable Artificial Intelligence (XAI) in recommendation systems. While many studies focus mainly on improving the technical performance of recommendation algorithms, fewer studies focus on how explanations can be presented in a way that is easily understood by users. This research contributes by proposing a user-centered framework that combines recommendation systems with explainability techniques. The study can also serve as a useful reference for future research on improving transparency and usability in AI-based systems.
1.5 Scope and Limitation 
 This study focuses on the development of a user-centric explainable artificial intelligence (XAI) framework for black-box e-commerce recommendation systems. The research aims to design an implement a simple recommendation system that can generate product recommendations and provide explanations that helps users understand why those recommendations are made. 
The study will involve the design of a basic recommendation model using machine learning techniques and integration of an explainability method to generate understandable explanations for the recommendations. The framework will demonstrate how explainable AI can improve transparency and user understanding in recommendation systems. 
The implementation will be carried out using a suitable dataset representing user interactions with products. The system will generate recommendations and provide explanations that highlights the factors influencing the recommendations. The study will mainly focus on demonstrating how explainability can improve user understanding rather than building a full commercial e-commerce platform. The limitations include:
The study focuses on a prototype recommendation system and will not implement a complete large-scale e-commerce platform. Therefore, the result may not fully represent the complexity of recommendation systems used by large online businesses. 
The framework will be implemented using a limited dataset, which may not cover all possible user behavior and product categories found in real-world e-commerce systems. 
The study will focus on a selected explainability techniques to generate explanations for recommendations. Other explainability methods may produce different results but are outside the scope of this research. 
Time and resource constraints may limit the extent of system testing and evaluation. However, the study will still provide a useful demonstration of how explainable AI can improve transparency and understanding in recommendation systems.

CHAPTER TWO 
LITERATURE REVIEW
2.1 Review of Key Concepts 
2.1.1 E-commerce Recommendation Systems 
The rapid expansion of digital commerce has significantly transformed the retail industry, making recommendation systems a central component of modern e-commerce platforms. Recommendation systems analyze user behavior, preferences, and product attributes in order to provide personalized product suggestions that enhance user experience and increase sales. A personalized product recommendation system leverages artificial intelligence to achieve tailored suggestions, significantly addressing the limitations of traditional, manual filtering methods (Li, 2024). These systems play an important role in improving user satisfaction and engagement by presenting products that are most relevant to a user’s interests.
2.1.2 Black-Box Machine Learning Models
Many modern recommendation systems rely on deep learning models and other complex machine learning techniques that operate as black-box systems. In such systems, the internal decision-making process of the algorithm is not easily interpretable by humans. Although these models often achieve high predictive accuracy, their lack of transparency makes it difficult for users and developers to understand how specific recommendations are generated. This lack of interpretability can severely reduce user confidence in automated recommendations. In domains where users rely on algorithmic suggestions to make purchasing decisions, the inability to understand why a recommendation was made can create skepticism toward the system.
2.1.3 Explainable Artificial Intelligence (XAI)
Explainable Artificial Intelligence (XAI) emerged as a solution to address the limitations of black-box machine learning models by providing interpretable explanations of algorithmic decisions. XAI techniques aim to reveal the reasoning behind predictions made by complex models, enabling both developers and users to understand how different input features influence the system's output. Integrating XAI into recommender systems is highly critical for improving user trust and engagement within the e-commerce domain (Sahu & Kaur, 2024). Furthermore, synergizing machine learning with XAI enhances customer interaction by providing transparency and addressing growing concerns regarding fairness and algorithmic accountability (Jensen & Abbas, 2024).
2.1.4 Model Agnostic Explanation Techniques 
Model-agnostic explanation techniques are widely used in explainable AI because they can interpret predictions generated by any machine learning model regardless of its internal structure. Two of the most commonly used techniques are Local Interpretable Model-Agnostic Explanations (LIME) and SHapley Additive exPlanations (SHAP). LIME works by approximating the behavior of a complex model locally using a simpler interpretable model. SHAP, on the other hand, is based on cooperative game theory and calculates the contribution of each feature using Shapley values. These techniques are widely adopted in modern explainable AI systems because they provide interpretable insights without requiring modifications to the underlying machine learning model.
2.1.5 User-Centric Explainability
Recent research in explainable recommender systems highlights the importance of designing explanations that are understandable to end users rather than focusing solely on technical interpretability. User-centric explainability emphasizes presenting explanations in ways that improve comprehension, usability, and trust. As a result, modern research increasingly focuses on combining mathematical explanations with human-readable descriptions that allow non-technical users to understand the reasoning behind algorithmic recommendations.
2.2 Theoretical Framework 
2.2.1 Human-AI Trust and Transparency
 The theoretical foundation for evaluating explainable recommendation systems is closely linked to the concept of human-AI trust. In online retail environments, ensuring that AI-driven recommendations are transparent and understandable to users is directly correlated with enhanced customer trust and satisfaction (Javaid et al., 2022). Transparent systems allow users to evaluate recommendations and determine whether they align with their preferences and expectations. Consequently, explainable AI techniques serve as an important mechanism for improving user trust and system adoption. 
2.2.2 Future Attribution and Mathematical Explainability 
One of the main approaches used to achieve explainability in machine learning systems is feature attribution. Akhtar (2024) demonstrated the theoretical viability of this by integrating feature attribution techniques like Integrated Gradients (IG) and DeepLIFT into a hybrid e-commerce system. Akhtar’s evaluation revealed that 82% of participants perceived recommendations accompanied by mathematical feature explanations as more trustworthy. However, while techniques like DeepLIFT effectively highlight feature importance for developers, exposing raw mathematical attributions to everyday shoppers often introduces cognitive overload. 
2.2.3 Hybrid Natural Language Explanation Framework 
While mathematical explanations provide valuable insights for developers, they may still be difficult for ordinary users to interpret. To address this limitation, researchers have proposed hybrid explainability frameworks that translate numerical explanations into natural language descriptions.
This approach was successfully codified by Rahim and Basheer (2025), who developed a hybrid explainability framework utilizing SHAP integrated with natural language interpretations. Their framework established that passing raw SHAP feature scores through a language generation layer to produce human-readable text yields highly positive user trust scores on a 5-point Likert scale. Their 2025 architecture serves as the primary theoretical anchor for this study's methodology.
2.3 Conceptual Framework 
The conceptual framework for this study integrates the theoretical principles of explainable artificial intelligence with the practical requirements of e-commerce recommendation systems. 
While existing systems like Akhtar's (2024) successfully applied XAI to e-commerce, they focused predominantly on seller recommendations and utilized technical outputs (DeepLIFT heatmaps) that are not consumer-friendly. Conversely, while Rahim and Basheer (2025) successfully translated SHAP data into intuitive natural language, their conceptual architecture was limited exclusively to the domain of career and aptitude recommendations.
Therefore, this project's conceptual framework introduces a novel intersection: it adapts the highly successful SHAP-to-Text translation pipeline proposed by Rahim and Basheer (2025) and engineers it specifically for product-level e-commerce recommendations. CHAPTER THREE
METHODOLOGY
This chapter describes the research methodology used in the development of the proposed user-centric explainable recommendation system for e-commerce platforms. It presents the research design, system architecture, system components, development tools, the implementation process, and the specific metrics used to evaluate the system’s effectiveness.
3.1 Research Design 
This research adopts a Design and Development research approach, focusing on engineering a functional system that integrates Explainable Artificial Intelligence (XAI) techniques into a digital retail environment.
To accurately measure the impact of the XAI integration, the research design incorporates a comparative A/B testing methodology. The system will be evaluated across two distinct user experiences:
The Control Group (Black-Box model): A standard e-commerce interface providing product suggestions without underlying explanations, establishing a baseline for user trust.
The Experimental Group (Hybrid XAI Model): The proposed user-centric system that pairs product recommendations with natural language justifications generated via feature attribution techniques.
This design allows the study to empirically measure the psychological and functional differences between opaque algorithms and transparent, user-centric AI.
System Architecture 
The proposed system follows a modular, multi-tier architecture that seamlessly integrates backend recommendation generation with frontend explainability mechanisms. The architecture is designed as a sequential pipeline consisting of five interconnected stages:
Data Input Layer: Ingestion of user and product datasets.
Recommendation Engine: The black-box predictive model.
Explainability Module: Mathematical feature attribution.
Natural Language Generator (NLG): The translation layer.
User Interface (UI): The frontend presentation layer.
This pipeline ensures that raw mathematical outputs are processed, translated, and rendered into human-readable formats before reaching the end-user.
3.3 System Component
3.3.1 Data Input Layer 
The data input layer collects and structures the relevant information required for generating predictions. This encompasses user behavior logs (e.g., browsing history, past purchases) and product attributes (e.g., category, price, aggregate ratings). This structured data forms the foundation for both training the model and extracting feature importance.
3.3.2 Recommendation Engine 
The recommendation engine serves as the core predictive component. Utilizing deep learning or collaborative filtering algorithms, this engine analyzes patterns within the data input layer to predict which products a specific user is most likely to engage with. It outputs a ranked list of product suggestions, functioning entirely as a "black-box" process.
3.3.3 Explainability Module 
To resolve the opacity of the recommendation engine, the explainability module applies model-agnostic XAI techniques specifically SHAP (SHapley Additive exPlanations) and LIME (Local Interpretable Model-Agnostic Explanations). This module intercepts the engine's output and calculates the exact mathematical weight of each input feature, determining exactly which factors (e.g., price similarity or browsing history) drove the recommendation.
3.3.4 Natural Language Explanation Generator (NLG)
To bridge the gap between technical metrics and human comprehension, the NLG component acts as a translation layer. It converts the complex numerical scores produced by SHAP and LIME into intuitive textual justifications. For example, instead of displaying a feature weight graph, the NLG outputs: "This product was recommended because you recently viewed similar electronic items."
  3.3.5 User Interface Module 
The user interface serves as the interaction point between the consumer and the AI. It dynamically renders the e-commerce storefront, displaying the recommended product cards alongside their respective natural language explanations, optimizing the user experience for transparency and minimal cognitive load.
3.4 System Development Tools 
The development of the proposed system utilizes an array of industry-standard programming tools and libraries:
Python: The primary programming language used for backend logic, algorithm development, and data manipulation.
Machine Learning Libraries: Scikit-learn, TensorFlow, or PyTorch for training the core recommendation engine.
XAI Libraries: The official SHAP and LIME Python libraries for extracting feature attributions.
Web Frameworks: Modern web development frameworks (such as Streamlit, Flask, or React) combined with HTML/CSS to build the interactive user interface.
3.5 Implementation Process 
The physical development of the system will be executed in the following sequential stages:
Data Preparation: Relevant e-commerce datasets will be collected, cleaned, and preprocessed. Missing values will be handled, and categorical data will be encoded to ensure algorithmic usability.
Model Training: The recommendation engine will be trained on a partition of the prepared dataset to learn historical consumer patterns and achieve high predictive accuracy.
Recommendation Generation: The trained model will ingest new user profiles to generate raw, personalized product predictions.
Explanation Generation: The SHAP/LIME modules will calculate the feature attributions for the generated predictions, which the NLG layer will subsequently translate into human-readable text.
System Integration: The backend pipeline will be wired to the frontend UI, allowing users to interact with the fully functional explainable recommendation system.
3.6 System Evaluation 
To comprehensively validate the system, the evaluation phase will measure both the mathematical accuracy of the backend and the psychological impact of the frontend.
3.6.1 Algorithmic Performance Evaluation 
The predictive capability of the underlying recommendation engine will be tested using standard machine learning classification metrics:
Precision: The percentage of recommended products that are highly relevant to the user
Recall: The model's ability to successfully identify all relevant products.
F1-Score: The harmonic mean of precision and recall.
3.6.2 User-Centric Evaluation (Trust and Clarity)
Following the proven methodology of Rahim and Basheer (2025), the effectiveness of the explanations will be evaluated using a 5-point Likert scale survey administered to human testers. Participants will rate the system based on:
Clarity: "I understand why the system recommended this product to me."
System Intent: "I believe this recommendation aligns with my shopping preferences."
Perceived Trust: "I trust the system's recommendation enough to make a purchase."
The results from the Experimental Group (XAI UI) will be compared against the Control Group (Black-Box UI) to definitively quantify the increase in user trust and recommendation acceptance.
